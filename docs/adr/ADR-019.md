# ADR-019 — Never place an emergency call programmatically

Status: Accepted · 2026-08-04

## Context

The instinct in a safety app is "when it is bad enough, the app calls 112". Three
separate reasons say no (PRD §4.5, P-009):

1. **It breaks Advanced Mobile Location.** AML fires from the platform's own
   emergency-call path and pushes a precise fix to the PSAP out of band. It is
   strictly better than anything this app can transmit and it works when our
   servers are gone. Any wrapper, interception or programmatic placement risks
   the OS not classifying the call as an emergency call, which silently kills
   AML — turning the one call that mattered into a worse call.
2. **A silent call with no speaker is often deprioritised** by the PSAP, exactly
   as it should be.
3. **It is legally and ethically wrong.** No third-party app in India can
   reliably inject a call into a state PSAP, and generating unattended calls
   into ERSS-112 is not a thing to do experimentally.

## Decision

The app hands the user to the dialler with the number pre-filled —
`Linking.openURL('tel:112')` — and gets out of the way. The final press is
theirs, one tap away, on a full-screen 88 dp button that appears on the
subject's device *and* every family device at L3.

The same reasoning applies to every emergency number, not only 112.

Because AML cannot be assumed to have worked, the coordinates are always
displayed in huge type as well, for a human to read aloud.

## Alternatives

**Auto-dial on high confidence** — rejected. There is no confidence threshold
that makes the AML risk acceptable, because the failure is silent: nothing
reports that AML did not fire.

## Consequences

- **An unconscious person never reaches 112 through this system.** That is the
  real cost, it is severe, and it is the reason the trusted-neighbour tier
  exists — in India a vetted neighbour 400 m away beats an ambulance 25 minutes
  away. The system's job is to get a human to the phone, not to replace one.
- The last step always depends on a person tapping. Every gain in t0–t3 stops at
  a human decision, permanently.
- The button must never be wrapped "just to log the tap". No telemetry hop, no
  state machine, no await before the dialler opens — and the literal digits stay
  on screen after a failure, because the true floor is a person reading "112"
  and dialling it by hand.
- `mobile/test/routes.test.ts` asserts the emergency number is never dialled
  programmatically, so a future convenience feature fails the build.

## Implementation

- `mobile/src/ui/components/Call112Button.tsx` — the whole decision, stated at
  the top of the file
- `mobile/app/panic.tsx`, `mobile/app/incident/[id].tsx` — the L3 placements
- `mobile/app/medical-card.tsx` — the same rule extended to every emergency
  number on the break-glass card
- `mobile/src/ui/components/BigCoordinates.tsx` — coordinates in huge type,
  because AML is never assumed
- `mobile/test/routes.test.ts` — the build-failing guard
