# ADR-015 — Android Device Owner provisioning on every family-owned phone

Status: Accepted · 2026-08-04 · the reason T0 is Android-only (see ADR-011)

## Context

Everything else in this system is downstream of one question: **is the agent
still running?** On a normal Android install the answer is routinely no, and for
reasons the app cannot argue with — the OEM battery manager killed it, the OS
auto-revoked its permissions after 90 days of "unused", somebody force-stopped
it, somebody uninstalled it, a thief factory-reset the phone (PRD §5, P-004,
P-006, P-022, P-029, P-036; RISK-001, plotted at 0.9 likelihood).

## Decision

Every family-owned phone is provisioned as an Android **Device Owner**, which
requires a factory reset and takes about 15 minutes per phone (docs/03 §3.3).
That single act removes OEM battery kill, permission auto-revocation, safe-boot
escape, second-user escape, uninstall and factory reset from the list of things
that can silently disable the agent.

The lockdown ships in **two waves**, and the release path ships before either:

| | Applied | Reversible from software |
|---|---|---|
| **`release()`** | first, before anything | — it *is* the reversal |
| **Wave 1** | now | yes |
| **Wave 2** | after a month of fleet stability | no |

Wave 1: permission auto-grant, uninstall block, `DISALLOW_SAFE_BOOT`,
`DISALLOW_ADD_USER`, Factory Reset Protection.
Wave 2: `DISALLOW_FACTORY_RESET`, then `DISALLOW_DEBUGGING_FEATURES` — **last,
always**.

The order is not stylistic. `DISALLOW_DEBUGGING_FEATURES` removes ADB, and ADB
is the last escape hatch on a phone whose controller has a bug. Without it, a
crashing controller leaves a device that cannot be managed, cannot be reset
(wave 2 disallowed it) and cannot be recovered by a recovery-mode wipe (wave 1's
FRP policy blocks the unlock). That is a brick made of three of our own
decisions.

## Alternatives

**A standard app install** — rejected. It is the configuration in which RISK-001
is unmitigated, and no amount of foreground-service care compensates for an OEM
that kills the process anyway.

## Consequences

- **This is genuine power over another person's phone**, and it is the single
  most serious ethical liability in the project. It is why the family agreement
  (`docs/family-agreement.md`) is a hard prerequisite and not paperwork: every
  member must be told in plain language exactly what device management permits
  and must consent. Android's own managed-device notice must never be hidden.
- Adults get a **reduced policy set** — reliability restrictions only:
  permission auto-grant and uninstall block. No app suspension, no kiosk. A
  wellbeing limit an adult cannot switch off is control with a friendlier icon
  (PRD §1.4.3).
- Provisioning wipes the phone and requires skipping Wi-Fi and the Google
  account during setup. It cannot be done to a phone already in use without
  losing what is on it, and it cannot be done at all to a phone the family does
  not own (PRD §1.7 C1).
- Wave 2 is irreversible from a device you can no longer reach. `release()`
  exists and is guardian-authenticated, but it runs *on* the device — a phone
  that will not boot the app is past the point where it helps.
- **iPhones cannot do any of this** without Apple Business Manager and Automated
  Device Enrolment, which are impractical for a family. iOS therefore gets a
  materially reduced feature set, and T0 does not exist there at all: shipping
  an iOS stub would let the diagnostics screen report a capability that cannot
  exist, which is the exact dishonesty T0 is built to avoid.
- Play Store distribution becomes irrelevant. APKs are hosted and installed
  directly. That is mostly an upside — no policy review for the SMS,
  accessibility and overlay permissions this needs — and it means update
  delivery is now the family's own problem.
- R8 can strip a Device Owner class in a release build without any crash: the OS
  simply never delivers `LOCKED_BOOT_COMPLETED` to a class that is not there.
  The keep rules in `mobile/app.json` and the post-build dex verification in
  DELIVERY.md §1 exist because of this, not as ceremony.

## Implementation

- `mobile/modules/kavach-t0/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/kavacht0/DeviceOwnerConfigurator.kt`
  — `T0_PERMISSIONS`, `WAVE_1_RESTRICTIONS`, `WAVE_2_RESTRICTIONS`, the 30-day
  soak gate, FRP with an empty-account guard, `release()`
- `mobile/modules/kavach-t0/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/kavacht0/KavachDeviceAdminReceiver.kt`
- `mobile/modules/kavach-t0/android/src/main/res/xml/kavach_device_admin.xml`
- `mobile/modules/kavach-t0/android/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml` — the `:t0`
  process and the receivers Device Owner makes reliable
- `mobile/src/t0/native.ts` — `isDeviceOwner()` and the honest capability report
- `mobile/src/t0/watchdog.ts` — what you do for phones that are not provisioned
- `mobile/app/diagnostics.tsx` — surfaces the real state, never an assumed one
- `docs/03-Implementation-Guide.md` §3.3 — the per-phone provisioning procedure
