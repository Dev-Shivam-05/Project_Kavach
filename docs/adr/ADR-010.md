# ADR-010 — Geofences are evaluated on-device and their coordinates never leave it

Status: Accepted · 2026-08-04

## Context

A geofence is not a coordinate. It is the home address, the school, the office,
the temple, the clinic — and a year of crossings is the family's routine, which
is the single most dangerous dataset this system could hold (PRD §10.2, P-008).
Server-side geofencing requires uploading all of it.

## Decision

Geofences are Class A. They are stored on the device with full precision, they
are evaluated on the device, and they are **never synced**. A boundary crossing
emits exactly `{ geofenceId, transition }` — an opaque UUID and an enum, Class B
— and nothing else. The server orchestrates the response without ever learning
where "school" is.

Two evaluation rules carry the false-positive budget (P-002, FR-002):

1. **Hysteresis.** Enter and exit use different thresholds, widened by the fix's
   own accuracy. A stationary phone at a boundary with ±30 m noise would
   otherwise emit enter/exit/enter/exit all night.
2. **Coarse fixes are refused, not guessed.** A 2 km cell-tower fix cannot decide
   a 150 m fence. Evaluating it anyway is the classic cause of "your child left
   school" at 03:00.

## Alternatives

**Server-side geofencing** — rejected. It is the conventional design and it
requires the family to hand over every address that matters to them.

## Consequences

- No server-side proximity features. The server cannot answer "who is near the
  incident", cannot compute "is Mummy home", cannot match a trusted neighbour by
  actual distance without a coarse cell. Neighbour matching runs on the H3 r7
  cell (Class B), which is coarser and therefore worse.
- No server-side debugging. When a fence misbehaves, the evidence is on one
  phone. There is no query anyone can run.
- Fence state is per-device. A second phone belonging to the same person does
  not know the fences until the E2EE group state syncs them, and a lost phone
  loses its fences unless they are restored from that state.
- The rule needs defence in depth, because a single careless field addition
  breaks it permanently. Three separate guards exist:
  - `stripClassA()` in `mobile/src/net/api.ts` removes `lat`, `lon`,
    `corridorPoints`, `corridor`, `points` and `geofences` from every
    control-plane body rather than trusting them not to appear
  - `toWireCrossing()` is the only geofence function whose output may be
    transmitted
  - `mobile/test/presence.test.ts` asserts the wire crossing carries no
    coordinates, so a regression fails the build instead of leaking quietly
- Map tiles are the leak nobody thinks about: requesting a tile discloses
  where the family is to whoever serves it. `FamilyMapView` is built around
  that (docs/02 §10.2).
- Functional cost is close to zero. Privacy benefit is the largest single one
  available: the home address, the children's school, the workplace and ten
  years of routine are removed from the server permanently.

## Implementation

- `mobile/src/domain/geofence.ts` — evaluation, hysteresis, coarse-fix refusal,
  `toWireCrossing()`
- `mobile/src/domain/presenceService.ts` — evaluates fences locally; the
  coordinates never cross the wire
- `mobile/src/db/schema.ts` — `local_geofence`, and `journey.corridor_json`,
  both marked never-synced
- `mobile/src/db/repos.ts` — the Class A columns and why nothing reads them for
  upload
- `mobile/src/net/api.ts` — `stripClassA()`
- `mobile/src/core/types.ts` — Class A annotation on the coordinate fields
- `mobile/app/(tabs)/map.tsx` — the rule stated where a family can read it
- `mobile/test/presence.test.ts` — the coordinate-leak guard
